Mac2Seven 1.3 adds **Snip**, a screenshot tool that works like the Windows Snipping Tool.

## Download
If you have 1.1 or later, Mac2Seven will offer this update on its own (or choose **Check for Updates…** from the menu bar icon's right-click menu). Otherwise, download **Mac2Seven.dmg** below, open it, and drag **Mac2Seven** into your Applications folder. Requires **macOS 14 Sonoma or later**. Signed with a Developer ID and notarized by Apple.

## New in 1.3: Snip
Press **⌥⇧S**, click the **scissors** next to the taskbar clock, or choose **Mac2Seven Snip** at the top of All Programs. The screen dims and a toolbar appears at the bottom.

- **Rectangle:** drag to select an area. Drag the middle to move it or the edges to resize it, then press **Capture** or Return. Next time, your last rectangle is already drawn.
- **Window:** click any window to capture it, with or without its shadow.
- **Full screen:** captures the display the pointer is on, taskbar included.
- **Freeform:** draw any shape and drag it into place. Outside the shape is transparent.
- **Timer:** wait 3, 5 or 10 seconds before capturing, for menus that close when you click away.

After a capture the image is **copied to the clipboard** (ready to paste), **saved** to **Pictures › Screenshots**, and **opened in Preview** for markup, with a thumbnail in the corner. Change any of this, the shortcut, or the folder in **Settings → Screenshots**.

Snip uses the same Screen Recording permission as the taskbar's window previews, so there's nothing new to allow. Your Mac's own ⌘⇧3, ⌘⇧4 and ⌘⇧5 still work as before.
